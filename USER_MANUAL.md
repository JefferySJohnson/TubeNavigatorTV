# TubeNavigatorTV — User Manual (v1.19 (20), 24 Nov 2026)

A Tube status board for the Apple TV. It does one thing: show the Tube status on the telly, big enough to read from the couch.

## The seven tabs

- **Status** — London weather at the top, then one button per line — all thirteen on one screen, as a clean list. Each button shows the line name, a coloured dot, and the status (Good Service in green, anything else in orange). Tap a button to see the full disruption message on its own screen; the "‹ Back to all lines" button at the bottom takes you back to the grid. At the top of the tab is a header row: the tab's title on the left, and the Refresh button with the time of the last successful check ("Updated HH:mm") at the upper right. Under the weather there is a forecast strip (day, icon, high and low); with it on, the bottom of the grid needs a small scroll down to see it, and with it off everything fits on one screen. **Press the forecast strip to change what it shows** — it cycles 7 days → next 12 hours → today (condition, high/low, rain chance, sunrise/sunset) — with a small hint underneath telling you what the next press shows. The view you pick sticks, so it is still there next time you open the app.
- **Planned** — dated disruptions (strikes and engineering works) for the next 7 days, or for the trip (Dec 15–24), grouped by day with the times in London time and the full reason. Switch ranges with the two buttons under the header row: move down to them from the tab bar, then left or right to the one you want and press to choose. **The next 7 days is the default** — the trip range is there when you want it.
- **Live** — six central London traffic cameras: Westminster Bridge, Parliament Square, Piccadilly Circus, Trafalgar Square, Oxford Circus, Tower Bridge. Each row shows the camera's picture and "Tap to watch". Press a camera to play the latest clip — about 11 seconds, refreshed every few minutes, and it loops while you watch. It is a moving snapshot, not a continuous stream, and the picture is small (352×288), so it looks soft on the big TV. There are no cameras inside Tube stations — TfL doesn't publish those. The "‹ Back to live views" button takes you back to the list.
- **Map** — the Tube diagram, with the thirteen line buttons beside it. Move through the buttons and the line you are on lights up on the map, with its live status in a panel underneath. The diagram is the September 2026 TfL map, so if TfL redraws it the picture goes out of date (the status stays live).
- **Boards** — departure boards, like the ones in the stations. First pick a station — Paddington, Canary Wharf, King's Cross St. Pancras, Liverpool Street, London Bridge, Victoria or Waterloo — and the board opens full screen, with the lines across the top. The board shows the next five trains on each platform: where each train is headed (with "via" on the Elizabeth line), how many minutes away it is, the arrival clock time in London time next to the minutes ("60 mins 05:48"), and where the train is now. A train at a minute or less reads "arriving". At Paddington + Elizabeth line both platforms (A on top, then B) fit on one screen. At Paddington the Elizabeth line board shows only platforms A and B — the mainline station's platforms 11 and 12, which carry late-night trains ending at Paddington, are left out (v1.18). It refreshes itself every 30 seconds, so you can leave it on and watch the times come down. Move left or right across the line buttons to switch lines — you don't need to press, the board follows the focus. Press the remote's Back button to return to the station list. Some boards are longer than one screen (Liverpool Street Elizabeth line has four platform groups, Canary Wharf DLR has six) — they scroll, with the station name and line buttons staying pinned at the top. Only Tube, Elizabeth line and DLR trains appear — mainline trains (Great Western Railway, Heathrow Express) are not in this TfL feed, so they never show. **Sound:** when a train on the open board first drops to a minute or less, the TV plays a soft bong once for that train. It never sounds when you first open a board. Turn it on or off, and pick the sound (Bong, Chime, Bell), in Settings.
- **Live Map** — the Elizabeth line, live, on a map. A redrawn schematic of the line (Reading and the Heathrow branches on the left, Shenfield and Abbey Wood on the right, all 41 stations as dots, the big stations named) with one dot per train on it right now — red heading east, blue heading west. Move onto a dot and the strip underneath tells you where that train is going, the stretch it is on, its next stop and how many minutes away ("To Paddington · next stop Manor Park in ~2 min" / "Between Ilford and Manor Park"). Left and Right move to the next train along the line; Up switches to the northern branch and Down to the southern branch. If there is no train that way, focus stays where it is. Press the clickpad to pin a train — the strip stays on it (with a "Pinned" badge) while you look at other trains, and it follows the train as it moves; press again to let it go. The strip also says how many trains are on the line when no train is focused. It refreshes itself every 30 seconds, like the boards. **The dots are estimates, and the screen says so:** the Elizabeth line feed never says where a train actually is, so each dot is placed between stations from the minutes to its next stop — close, but not exact. Only the Elizabeth line is on this tab; the other lines are on the Map tab.
- **Settings** — the last tab, gear icon. On the left: the app icon, the build version ("TubeNavigatorTV v1.19 (20)"), and where the data comes from. On the right: **Theme** — four dark schemes (Midnight, Tube Navy, Oxblood, Forest) that tint the whole app; there is no light theme, because the white focus highlight is drawn by the TV itself and a light background would hide it. **Weather** — a "Show forecast" switch that turns the forecast strip on the Status tab on or off. **Sound** — an "Arrival sound" switch (on by default) and the sound picker: Bong (the default), Chime, or Bell.

There is no journey planning, no saved places or pubs — that is all in the iPhone app. The TV app is for glancing at.

## Using the remote

The remote moves focus; it does not tap. Move up, down, left and right with the clickpad (or the arrow keys on a paired keyboard), and press the clickpad to choose whatever is focused.

- Move up to the tab bar, then left or right to switch tabs — switching is immediate, you do not need to press.
- On the Status tab, move down, left and right through the line buttons. Press to open a line's full message; press "‹ Back to all lines" at the bottom of the detail to return to the grid. Press the forecast strip to change what it shows (7 days, hours, today).
- On the Planned tab, move up to the range control to switch between "Next 7 days" and "Trip · Dec 15–24", then move down through the days.
- On the Live tab, move down through the cameras and press to watch one; press "‹ Back to live views" at the bottom of the clip to return to the list.
- On the Map tab, move down through the line buttons; the map lights up the line you are on.
- On the Boards tab, move down to the station list and press a station to open its board. Move left or right across the line buttons at the top to switch lines — the board follows the focus, no press needed. Press the remote's Back button to return to the station list. The board refreshes itself every 30 seconds.
- On the Live Map tab, move down from the tab bar onto the trains. Left and Right follow the line to the next train; Up switches to the northern branch and Down to the southern branch. If there is no train that way, focus stays put. The focus pill rings the train you are on and the strip underneath describes it. Press to pin a train (the strip stays on it, with a "Pinned" badge); press again to release it. The map refreshes itself every 30 seconds. Note: on this tab the Refresh button at the upper right is not reachable by remote — Up from a train goes to the tab bar, and Down from the tab bar skips Refresh and lands on a train — so the 30-second refresh is how it stays current. (The Refresh button on the other tabs is reached by moving up from the top row of content, as below.)
- To refresh a tab by hand, move up from the top row of content — Refresh sits at the upper right — and press it. (Down from the tab bar goes straight to the content and skips Refresh; up from the content is how you reach it.)
- Press Menu (Back) to leave the tab bar; on a detail screen, use the "‹ Back to all lines" button.

## Weather

The weather is fixed to Paddington — the hotel (4 North Wharf Road) — because the Apple TV has no user location to use. It shows the current temperature and condition (with its own little icon), today's high and low, a forecast strip (turn it off in Settings if you want the whole Status tab on one screen), and "Data from Apple Weather" underneath. Press the strip to switch it between the 7-day forecast, the next 12 hours, and today's detail (condition, high/low, rain chance, sunrise and sunset) — the view you pick is remembered.

If it reads "Weather isn't available right now", the App ID has not been switched on for WeatherKit in the developer portal yet — the same step that was done for the iPhone app. The Tube status still works without it. (As of 22 Nov 2026 the weather is live, so this step is done.)

## Putting it on the real Apple TV

The app is verified in the Apple TV 4K simulator. To install it on the real Apple TV, pair the TV with Xcode over the network (on the TV: Settings → Remotes and Devices → Remote App and Devices; in Xcode: Window → Devices and Simulators), pick the TV as the run destination, and run. Signing is the same as the iPhone app — your team, automatic signing. (v1.3 was run to the Samsung 55 TV on 22 Nov 2026; on the morning of 23 Nov 2026 Jeffery reported the TV he's using shows v1.13 (14), and later that morning he installed v1.14 (15) on the Samsung 55 himself — so v1.14 is on a real TV now. v1.15 and v1.16 are simulator-only until he installs them. v1.16 is on the Samsung 55 — Jeffery reported 23 Nov 2026 ~12:23 EST that he has 1.16 loaded on the Samsung 55, maybe another TV too. v1.19 was built and verified on the simulator on 24 Nov 2026, then installed by Jeffery on the projector Apple TV; he checked the Live Map navigation early morning London time with about 20 trains on the line and it looked correct. The Samsung 55 still has v1.16 until Jeffery builds v1.19 to it from the MacBook Pro.)

## What it needs

- An Apple TV 4K (or any tvOS 17+ Apple TV) and a network connection. TfL status is live data; without a connection the app shows its error text instead of stale lines.
